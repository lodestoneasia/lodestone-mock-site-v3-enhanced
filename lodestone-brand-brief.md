## 2.1 Positioning (important)

Lodestone is **not** a "special needs centre." It is a personalisation centre that happens to be especially well-equipped for neurodivergent children. The phrasing matters:

- **Do say:** "personalised learning for every child", "we specialise in personalisation", "neurodivergent, gifted, and everyone between"
- **Don't say:** "centre for special needs", "centre for children with learning differences" (as the only descriptor), or anything that implies the centre is only for diagnosed children.

The audience we lose by leading with "learning differences only" is: parents of neurotypical kids who'd benefit from the math, coding, IGCSE, or SEL programs; parents of gifted kids who need more challenge; parents who suspect their child needs support but aren't comfortable with a diagnostic frame.

## 3. What we believe (the four principles)

The centre is built on four commitments that shape every program:

1. **Etiquette · Virtue · Order**, Japanese-inspired foundations. Clean habits, kind hearts, quiet order. Children learn how to be, not just what to do. This phrase appears on the logo wordmark and is the centre's philosophical north star.

2. **Evidence-based methods.** Orton-Gillingham–informed literacy, multi-sensory math, neuroaffirming SEL, each taught by educators trained in that specific method, never improvised.

3. **Progress, tailored to your child.** Some children need weekly check-ins; others a monthly summary at a milestone. Cadence, format, and focus are shaped by your child's profile, never a one-size-fits-all template.

4. **Family-coached, school-linked.** With parents' permission, we coordinate with the child's school, OT, or psychologist. Parents get session debriefs, you stay in the loop.

## 5. Lodestone Evo, the early-intervention program

A Japanese-inspired program for **ages 2–6** (supported through 8). Built on five virtues, practiced daily, never lectured:

- **清潔 Seiketsu**, Cleanliness
- **躾 Shitsuke**, Discipline / proper conduct
- **和 Wa**, Harmony
- **頑張る Ganbaru**, Perseverance
- **思いやり Omoiyari**, Compassion / consideration for others

Evo runs 4 hours daily and is foundational rather than remedial. Children learn how to live, learn, and relate, through routine, reflection, and gentle academic readiness.

## 6. The intake process

1. **Connect**, Free 15-min call. We listen to concerns and goals.
2. **Assess**, Comprehensive screening to understand the child's learning profile.
3. **Plan**, A personalized program built around strengths, paced for sustainable growth.
4. **Grow**, Ongoing sessions, progress reports (flexible cadence), and family coaching.

**Important:** No diagnosis is required to begin. Many families start with a screening so we can understand the child's profile before deciding on a path.

## 7. Audience psychology

Our audience is a mix of neurodivergent parents, parents of gifted learners, and parents of children who simply need personalised support. They buy unlike almost any other audience:

- They research obsessively, 12–30+ pages before deciding
- They are emotionally heavy. They worry their child is being judged.
- They trust other parents far more than they trust marketing
- They have often been told "wait and see" or "they'll grow out of it", and they no longer believe it
- They want to be told their child has gifts, not problems
- They are wary of being sold to

**Our job, always, is to lower their shoulders.** Speak to them like a wise friend who has seen many children flourish, not like a professional selling a service.

## 8. Brand voice & tone

**Warm. Humanist. Editorial. Confident, but never salesy.**

The voice belongs to a thoughtful, experienced educator who has seen many children and trusts the process. Quiet authority, never volume.

### Always do

- Lead with **strengths**, not problems. "Your child has a remarkable visual memory, let's channel that into reading."
- Use **specific** language. "Eight to ten weeks of clearer articulation" beats "we'll help your child speak better."
- Honour the parent's research. They've already read. Don't condescend.
- Be honest about timelines. Don't promise overnight transformation.
- Use editorial pacing, short sentences, contemplative beats. Prefer commas, periods, and colons over dashes.
- Speak to the child as a whole person, not a diagnosis.

### Never do

- Don't say "**fix**", "**cure**", "**treat**", "**suffering from**", "**at-risk**"
- Don't say "**special needs**", say "learning differences" or "neurodivergent learners"
- Don't say "**patient**", "**case**", "**subject**", say "child", "family", "learner"
- Don't use clinical jargon when plain English works
- Don't use exclamation points to manufacture enthusiasm
- Don't use emoji in formal contexts (occasionally `👋` `✨` `🌱` are fine on social media)
- Don't ever imply the child is broken or deficient

### Vocabulary preferences

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Special needs | Learning differences / neurodivergent |
| Disorder | Difference |
| Treatment | Program / support |
| Fix / cure | Help / channel / build |
| Suffering from | Living with / experiencing |
| Patient | Child / learner |
| Diagnosis-driven | Strengths-first |
| Behaviour problem | Regulation challenge |
| Slow learner | Different-paced learner |

## 11. Boundaries & integrity

- **No medical claims.** We are not a clinical practice. We do not diagnose. We screen and we educate.
- **No comparison to clinics.** Don't position against psychiatrists, paediatricians, or therapists. We complement them.
- **No before/after photos** of children. Ever. Anonymized stories with the parent's words only.
- **No promised outcomes.** "Confidence often shifts within weeks; measurable academic progress typically shows in 8–12 weeks" is acceptable. "Your child will read by Tuesday" is not.
- **Always offer the free 15-min call** as the first action. Never push a screening or program as the first step.
